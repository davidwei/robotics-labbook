# SO-101 follower 12 V motor replacement — 2026-10-24

The operator returned to the lab and reported completing replacement of the SO-101 follower motors so that every follower joint now uses a 12 V motor variant. This is a hardware-maintenance milestone following the 2026-10-23 session, where the installed motor variants were unresolved and diagnostic voltage readings were approximately 4.7–5.1 V.

Read-only register inspection found 12.2–12.3 V at all six follower motors and 5.1–5.2 V at all six leader motors. Torque was disabled and status was zero throughout the audit. The follower motors shared 4.0–14.0 V configured limits. Five leader motors reported a 12.0 V upper limit while `wrist_roll` reported 8.0 V; this is a configuration observation, not proof of either motor variant. No commanded motion occurred.

The operator identified both saved leader and follower calibration records as stale and recalibrated both arms interactively. The exact before/after JSON files are archived and hashed. All six motor IDs remained 1–6 on both arms. The follower offsets changed substantially after motor replacement; captured spans changed by +50, −16, −116, −309, 0 and −83 counts in joint order. These are calibration observations, not evidence that tracking or recovery behavior is validated.

The controlled repeat reached a powered preflight but is now blocked. The runner entered `Idle` while commanding the saved folded rest pose, then latched a follower `elbow_flex` status 32 load-overload fault about 2.1 seconds later, before Tracking or directional testing began. Immediately before the fault, the elbow measured 103.34 degrees while the saved rest command was 94.46 degrees and the calibrated upper bound was 94.90 degrees. All other motor statuses remained zero. The runner was stopped and its complete zero-drop telemetry is archived. See [repeat-preflight.md](repeat-preflight.md).

After reviewing the difference from upstream teleoperation, the supervised runner was changed so the periodic motor monitor is report-only: nonzero status or unexpected torque-enable values remain in telemetry and produce console warnings, but no longer drive the controller into `FAULT` or suppress leader commands. The change is preserved in local code revision `c5ce05ec2e649132f5546631f108b02fa7d23c37`; 60 focused SO-101 tests and Ruff passed. This software decision does not resolve the elbow overload or make the physical repeat ready.

A subsequent torque-off read found status zero on every motor, showing that the elbow's status-32 flag did not persist after shutdown. The unloaded elbow measured 96.04 degrees, still 1.14 degrees beyond its 94.90-degree calibrated upper bound. The cleared bit therefore does not show that the endpoint/load condition has been corrected.

The report-only powered diagnostic reproduced the elbow overload without entering Tracking. Status was initially zero, then changed to 32 about 2.59 seconds after the first status poll and remained 32 for 22 consecutive polls through shutdown. The elbow drifted from 96.04 to 104.22 degrees while the measured-pose hold target remained 96.04 degrees; peak sampled load was -1000 and peak sampled current was 419 raw. One console warning was expected because warning display is edge-triggered, while telemetry preserves every poll. This establishes that the powered endpoint/load problem is reproducible even though the status bit clears while torque is off.

The operator clarified that the elbow calibration endpoint was intentionally set inside the arm's true geometric limit to provide collision margin, and that the folded rest pose may lie on or outside that conservative calibration boundary. Therefore, elbow positions above 94.90 degrees are not evidence by themselves that the arm contacted its geometric hard stop. The revised leading issue is a mismatch between the conservative software range and the selected folded rest, combined with reproducibly high holding effort in that configuration. Mechanical contact, motor phase/direction, horn alignment and calibration quality remain unverified rather than established causes.

The operator prefers to retain the current folded rest geometry. Future design work should therefore keep the conservative teleoperation boundary while treating the folded pose separately as a parked/rest configuration. The unresolved requirement is to avoid sustained elbow overload there, for example through mechanical support or explicit torque-off parking rather than redefining the preferred pose as an ordinary powered operating target.

For the next operator-requested teleoperation diagnostic, the `awaiting_alignment` pickup-exit tolerance was separated from the existing startup/tracking alignment threshold. Revision `c779a6c23cdc00694fe99243d874f60a916c193a` permits pickup exit when each rotary joint is within 15 degrees; the gripper remains at 3 normalized units, and startup/rest plus normal tracking alignment remain at their prior 1.5-degree/3-unit thresholds. Sixty-two focused tests and Ruff passed; physical validation is pending.

After upstream teleoperation appeared unable to move motor ID 3 (`elbow_flex`), a torque-off read-only register comparison found normal communication and no configuration outlier: model 777, firmware 3.10, phase 12, position mode, PID 16/0/32, 12.2 V and the principal protection settings matched the other non-gripper motors. The elbow was warmer at 42 C versus 31–34 C for the others. Its raw present position was 3145 and last goal 3135 while its configured maximum position limit was 3114. These observations do not prove motor health; an unloaded isolated motion test is required to distinguish motor/output failure from linkage, limit and load effects.

The operator then removed motor ID 3 from the elbow linkage and connected it as the only motor on the Waveshare board. A direct single-motor read confirmed ID 3, model 777, firmware 3.10, 12.2 V, status zero, torque disabled, zero current/load and temperature reduced to 37 C. The shaft remained at raw position 3145, above the configured maximum 3114. No isolated motion command has yet been issued.

The authorized unloaded isolated motion test passed. With torque limited to 200 raw, motor ID 3 moved inward from raw 3145 to the configured upper-limit region, then completed a 3114 to 3094 to 3114 small-step sequence in the correct directions. Status remained zero, current stayed at 0–2 raw, load stayed between -56 and 68 raw, voltage remained 12.2 V and temperature stayed 37–38 C. Torque was disabled and temporary control settings were restored after the test. This strongly supports that the motor, encoder, communications and unloaded output stage function; the earlier failure is now more likely associated with the installed linkage, horn/coupler geometry, assembly preload or required joint torque rather than an intrinsically nonmoving motor.

An authorized 45.01-degree unloaded inward rotation was then tested. The first attempt wrote the large target while torque was disabled; after torque enable, the goal register showed 2597 but the shaft remained at 3109 with near-zero current and status zero until the guarded timeout. The retry first enabled holding at the measured position, then wrote the same target while torque was active. It passed in 0.95 seconds, moving 3109 to 2602 toward target 2597 with current 0–7 raw, load 0–200 raw, status zero and temperature 37 C. Torque was disabled and temporary settings restored. This confirms substantial unloaded travel and documents that target/torque-enable ordering matters for this servo path.

The first operator-provided spare servo was then connected alone and identified as ID 4, model 777, firmware 3.10, with full raw limits 0–4095, phase 12, 12.4 V, torque disabled and status zero. A guarded unloaded comparison at torque limit 200 commanded raw 476 to 496 after establishing powered hold, but the shaft position remained 476 for 0.78 seconds while load increased to approximately -96 raw and current remained 0–2 raw. One temperature sample jumped from 26 to 59 C, triggering the abort; immediate torque-off recheck returned 26 C and status zero. The temperature jump is treated as transient/unconfirmed, but the absence of motion is a failed result. No 45-degree command was attempted on this spare.

The second spare was connected alone and identified as ID 5, model 777, firmware 3.10, with phase 12, 12.3 V, torque disabled, status zero and raw position 52. It failed both guarded unloaded commands at torque limit 200: the +20-count target held position 52 for two seconds with load reaching -172 raw, and the direct +512-count target produced only 50–53 raw position variation over five seconds while load reached the imposed -200 torque limit. Current remained 0–4 raw, status stayed zero and temperature samples remained 24–36 C. Torque was disabled and temporary settings restored after each attempt. Compared with motor 3's successful motion under the same torque limit, both spares exhibit abnormal nonmotion or substantially greater breakaway resistance; higher-torque testing has not been authorized or performed.

Motor ID 3 was reconnected alone and the same reduced-torque comparison was repeated. It passed raw 2601 to 2581, back to 2601, then to 2089 (45.01 degrees), reaching within five counts of each target in 0.17, 0.17 and 0.96 seconds. Current remained 0–10 raw, load -112 to 200 raw, status zero and temperature 32–33 C. Torque was disabled and temporary settings restored. This repeat strengthens the controlled contrast: motor 3 moves normally at torque limit 200, while both spares failed to move under that same limit.
