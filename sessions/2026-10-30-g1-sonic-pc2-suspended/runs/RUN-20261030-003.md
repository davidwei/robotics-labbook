# RUN-20261030-003 — tethered ground-contact IDLE and PICO stop

G1-29 stood with both soles on the floor and carried its normal weight while the
gantry acted only as a fall catch. After ground-contact initialization and a
stable approximately 71 Hz PICO stream, the operator executed the pre-agreed
start/count/stop chord sequence without waiting for an agent prompt between
chords. The manager logged exactly one OFF-to-PLANNER and one PLANNER-to-OFF
transition. SONIC entered IDLE planner control and exited normally after stop.

The operator reported that the robot stood firmly before the second chord and
that the gantry remained a catch rather than supporting it. The measured policy
interval was 12.28 seconds. Maximum leg-joint range was 0.613 rad at the left
knee; maximum overall measured joint speed was 6.26 rad/s at the left elbow.
Base roll/pitch/yaw ranges were approximately 3.8/9.5/9.0 degrees. No sampled
motor error/status, LowState/LowCmd CRC failure, recorder drop, or callback
failure occurred.

After the second chord, LowCmd publication stopped, no stock-controller command
resumed, and the robot could no longer stand. It relaxed into the gantry, matching
the previously observed development-mode behavior. The independent capture
retained 60.23 seconds after the last LowCmd; maximum measured post-stop joint
change was 0.359 rad at left ankle pitch. The PICO chord therefore demonstrated
a clean process/control stop, not a stand-preserving handover.

This run supports firm tethered ground-contact IDLE control during the active
phase. It does not validate stale-input response, locomotion, untethered balance,
or post-stop handover. See `RUN-20261030-003.yaml` for exact configuration,
metrics and evidence limitations.
